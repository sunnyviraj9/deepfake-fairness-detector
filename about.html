🎯 Research Motivation

Deepfake detection has achieved remarkable accuracy on balanced benchmarks, but real-world deployment surfaces a critical problem: models trained on demographically skewed data produce systematically higher false positive rates on darker-skinned faces. A real person wrongly flagged as a deepfake can face serious consequences in identity verification, content moderation, and media authentication.

FairShield XAI is inspired by the CVPR 2025 AI-Face FairnessBench paper, which first systematically evaluated deepfake detectors across the Monk Skin Tone Scale. Our work extends this by integrating fairness mitigation directly into the training loop via equalized odds regularisation, and by making the fairness gap a primary optimisation target alongside AUC.

The result is a model that achieves AUC 0.9987 and Monk F_EO = 0.0515 — a 71.4% reduction in demographic disparity from the unmitigated baseline — while maintaining near-perfect fake detection recall.

🗄️ Dataset & Data Pipeline
Real Faces — IMDB-Wiki

62k+ celebrity face images from the IMDB-Wiki dataset. Files stored under dataset/imdb_wiki/imdb/ and dataset/imdb_wiki/wiki/ in 100 subdirectories (two-digit prefix). Original images are pre-cropped face regions in JPEG format.

Real label: target=0
Fake Faces — Stable Diffusion 1.5

Synthetic faces generated from IMDB-Wiki subject metadata using Stable Diffusion 1.5. Stored under dataset/StableDiffusion1.5_wiki_processed_process/. PNG format, same 100-subdir structure. Visually indistinguishable from real faces.

Fake label: target=1
Demographic Auto-Labeling

scripts/auto_annotate_demographics.py extracts Lab L* channel (centre crop) and maps median luminance to MST 1–10 via 9 calibrated breakpoints. Gender uses a* channel heuristic. Hash-based fallback for unreadable images. All 4 annotation CSVs contain columns: image_path, target, gender, skin_tone, age.

skin_tone: 1-10 gender: Male/Female
⚙️ Training Pipeline
1. Data Loading — DeepfakeFairnessDataset
Custom PyTorch Dataset reads from CSV annotations. Train transforms: Resize(224) → RandomHorizontalFlip → RandomRotation(10°) → ColorJitter → ToTensor → Normalize. WeightedRandomSampler assigns weight=1/subgroup_count per sample when mitigate_bias=True.
2. Model Construction — build_model()
Factory function in models/__init__.py dispatches to SpatialDetector, FrequencyDetector, or DualStreamDetector. timm backbone initialised with pretrained=True (ImageNet). Custom head uses Xavier uniform init.
3. Fairness Loss — EqualizedOddsRegularizedLoss
Primary: BCEWithLogitsLoss. Penalty: λ × (Var(soft_FPR_g) + Var(soft_TPR_g)) where g iterates unique skin tone groups in the batch. Differentiable via sigmoid surrogates. λ=0.5 by default.
4. Optimisation — AdamW + CosineAnnealingLR
AdamW with lr=1e-4, weight_decay=0.01. Gradient clipping at max_norm=1.0. CosineAnnealingLR scheduler (T_max=epochs, eta_min=1e-6). Best checkpoint saved on val AUC improvement (includes monk_F_EO, optimal_threshold).
5. Threshold Calibration — find_optimal_threshold()
Post-training: walks the ROC curve selecting the highest TPR point with FPR ≤ 5%. Falls back to EER threshold if no point meets the budget. Result stored in checkpoint as optimal_threshold and read dynamically by app.py and evaluate.py.

⚖️ Fairness Framework — CVPR 2025 AI-Face FairnessBench

This project aligns with the evaluation protocol from the AI-Face FairnessBench paper (CVPR 2025), which identified that commercial and academic deepfake detectors exhibit systematic performance disparities across the Monk Skin Tone Scale. Key metrics used:

Equalized Odds Disparity (F_EO)
F_EO = 0.5 × (ΔFPR + ΔTPR)

ΔFPR = max(FPR_g) − min(FPR_g) across all subgroups g. ΔTPR = max(TPR_g) − min(TPR_g). Lower F_EO = fairer model. Our result: F_EO = 0.0515 (MST 1–10).

Monk Skin Tone Scale (MST 1–10)

A 10-point perceptual skin tone scale replacing the coarser Fitzpatrick scale. Light (1–3), Medium (4–7), Dark (8–10). Used for intersectional subgroup analysis combined with gender (20 total subgroups).
